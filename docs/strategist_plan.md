# Human-strategy net ("Strategist") - revised after adversarial review

## Problem
Train a net that has seen three human strategies (Producer: cheapest, widest seller; Showcase: most attractive and varied offer; Shipper: buy and ship to the Island) and can adopt or counter them.
It should be stronger than Peddler v2 and fun to play against. The strategies depend on each other, so the target is a population with a cycle, not three fixed bots.
All runs use the frozen Peddler rules (`--no-same-turn-repay --min-bid 3 --buy-at-harbor --bank-needs-load --no-double-reprice`; the last removes the repricing no-op). The ruleset is versioned and identical in training and evaluation.

## Principle: strategic identity is an objective, not an action restriction
Producer, Showcase and Shipper are economic philosophies (control the supply market, be the partner buyers choose, exploit the Island), not action sets. Every archetype keeps the whole legal action space; a Producer that buys a rival's stock, or a Shipper that produces, is welcome when it serves the objective. Rewards therefore pay the economic position (share of sales and sales money, colours buyers took, Island points), never an action such as producing, buying or shipping, and the evaluation sets no action quotas or fixed action distribution. Monopoly is not rewarded for itself: the win-slack floor and the pathology check guard against stock that is bought and never used.

## Phase 1 - Trainer change - done
- Generalise to `--shaping-objective {interaction,island,producer,showcase}`: one adaptive coefficient driven by the existing win-slack controller. The control law stays unchanged; add tests for a violated floor and for slack. `--shaping-weight` is only the starting value of the coefficient, which may rise to `--dual-max` (0.6); in Phase 2 every shaped run rose to it (see the findings). Composites use fixed relative weights; no multiple adaptive weights.
- Terms (`selfplay.shaping_term`): producer = share of the table's sales, share of its sales money, share of offers at $3 or less; showcase = colours buyers took from the seat, fair pricing, sales (`--showcase-variant variety|fair|sales`); island = Island points.
- Bids within the allowance, `--bid-within-value`: off by default, an ablation for the Shipper only. A bound seat may not have more on the table in a delivery auction (its bids so far, a runoff included) than the larger of two amounts: what the cargo is worth on its own secret scoring card (`ScoringCard::cargo_value`: the sum over the cargo's containers of their value on the card, which counted from the card's $10 colour is $10, $10, $6, $4 and $2, with a full set of colours assumed, so the second colour is $10, and no colour discarded, whatever island the cargo would join), and $5 (`BID_ALLOWANCE_FLOOR`). It is a nominal value, not the marginal gain, so a cargo of the colour that would be the discarded pile is still worth its card value; the marginal gain can exceed it when the cargo completes a set or changes which colour is discarded. Why the floor: the table's minimum bid ($3) does not give way to the cap, so without it a cargo worth under $3 (in practice one container of the card's last colour, $2) would leave no legal bid and the engine would waive all rules for that decision (it never leaves a seat without a move). The floor lifts the cap for every cargo worth under $5. The history of the rule is in the next bullet. Set `BID_ALLOWANCE_FLOOR` to 0 to drop it, and relax the minimum bid to the cap if both rules are bound.
- History of the cap, in four forms; forms 2, 3 and 4 were each the user's change, with the reason given. (1) `--max-bid 5`, a fixed cap on a delivery bid that the implementer added as the Shipper ablation, to test whether overbidding drives the Shipper's result (the money loop of README open item 2). The user rejected it as meaningless: a seat should not be able to bid more dollars than the cargo gives it on its island. Runs under it: island points +1.50 / +1.65 SD, win 0.144 / 0.141 (checkpoints in `runs/strategist_superseded/`). (2) The marginal gain of the cargo on the bidder's own island (`island_gain`), with the minimum bid giving way to it. The user asked whether the first bid is then always $0: yes for a first single-colour cargo, because its colour is the discarded pile and adds nothing to the island, so such a seat could bid only $0. (3) The larger of the marginal gain and $5, the user's fix. The capped Shippers were retrained and gated under it: island points +1.82 / +1.90 SD, win 0.180 / 0.228. (4) The nominal card sum with the $5 floor (current), at the user's request: "the sum value of the container on the scoring card, even if the most is discarded, and assuming 5 color", without retraining. That request did not say whether the $5 floor of form 3 stays; the implementer kept it because of the minimum-bid conflict above (open question 7). Forms 1 to 3 no longer exist in the code (`max_bid`, `island_gain`), so nothing trained under them can be re-evaluated under its own rule; only the current rule can be run.
- Warm start with new inputs: zero-initialised extra input columns; check the `features.rs`/export format.

## Phase 2 - Archetype experiments (2 seeds each, from `peddler2.pt`, 30 min)
- Producer, Shipper (uncapped, and bound to the bid allowance as ablation), Showcase in 3 variants (variety only, variety + fair price, variety + sales), and a no-shaping control.
- **Behavioural audit (report only, not pass/fail)**: per archetype, produced, bought, sold and shipped containers and how they vary with the situation (rival prices, colours missing), to see whether cross-strategy behaviour emerges. No minimum purchase or production rates.
- **Divergence gate**, per archetype, against Peddler v2 on matched seats: primary metric + at least one corroborating metric (Producer: sales share / sell-through and competitive offers; Showcase: colours buyers took / competitive colours available; Shipper: shipped rate / deliveries and economic contribution). At least 300 games per seat, stratified bootstrap, no action exclusivity required, effect size >= 0.2 (recalibrated on Phase 0 spread) with 95% CI excluding zero.
- Pathology check, independent: no production without sales, variety without purchases, or deliveries that hurt the result. The win floor is checked separately.
- If no archetype passes: stop and rethink rewards. Showcase is kept only if it helps commercially. Passing archetypes get extra seeds.

**Findings after the gate.** Written while verifying the plan against the code and the logs; the evidence tags are explained under Status.
- **The naive expectations against the data.** The user expected Producers to build as many factories as they can, nets with harbors to build warehouses, and Shippers to load 3-5 containers a boat. *Measured* (`move_table.md`, 100 games per seat, 181-209 moves a game): factories are 0.5% of Peddler v2's moves, none of Consortium's or Magnate's, 0.7% / 0.5% of the Producers' (the controls 0.4% / 0.5%), 0.1% or less of the Shippers' and 0.5-0.8% of the Showcases'; 0.5% is about one factory a game (derived from rounded shares), so the Producers build no more than the unshaped controls. No net builds a warehouse (`shipper_s1` under 0.1%). Loading at the Bank is 1.8-3.4% of the moves of Peddler v2 and the trained nets; Consortium and Magnate never sail to the Bank. *Ad hoc* (`delivery_table`, 40 games per seat, stalled games excluded, not saved in any report): every net measured sails to the Island with 1.00-1.12 containers, although a ship holds 5; a smoke run of 20 games per seat gave 1.01 (Peddler v2) and 1.14 (Magnate). The gate's deliveries per game agree (9.3-11.6 against Peddler v2's 10.0). *Revised by later probes* (Economic probes, "Is the pretrained start the problem?"): the sentence on Producers and controls holds at this table only; at a table of Consortium, Magnate and the scripted Merchant the Producers build 1.0-1.3 factories a game against 0.35-0.60 for the controls, because the control's building depends on who else sells.

- **Why the nets may not build or fill ships (all *hypothesis* when written; the Economic probes below tested some of them).** No reward pays a build, and a build burns cash; its payoff is delayed; every run starts from Peddler v2, which builds in 0.5% of its moves, and the anchors build none, so builds are rarely sampled; with one warehouse each a harbor holds one container, so a harbor stop adds at most one container to the ship and a cargo of k needs about k stops (a Bank load is the other way to fill the ship). *Source*, the rules behind it: from $20 of starting cash, the 2nd to 4th factory cost $6 / $9 / $12 (at most 4, one per colour) and the 2nd to 5th warehouse $4 / $5 / $6 / $7, burned to the supply; production needs $1 for wages, is allowed once per turn and makes one container per factory up to the free room (2 per factory); a harbor holds one container per warehouse, a purchase at a harbor goes onto the ship, an empty ship may not sail to the Island, and a one-container loop is Ocean, harbor, Ocean, Island, so each extra container costs about 2 more sails unless it is loaded at the Bank.
- **What the data does not show.** Whether building or full ships would pay in this ruleset: nobody computed whether a 5-container trip pays more than five single trips, or whether building early scores higher. Containers per delivery is a mean; with an empty ship barred from the Island, at most (mean - 1) / (k - 1) of the deliveries carry k or more containers, so at most 6% of Magnate's deliveries and 0.5% of Peddler v2's carry 3 or more.
- **Island points belong to the bidder side.** *Source*: in an accepted delivery the deliverer gets twice the top bid in cash (the bid plus an equal sum minted from the supply) and the cargo goes to the winning bidder's scoring area; a buy-out keeps the cargo with the deliverer but is under 0.1% of every net's moves. A net's Island points are therefore containers it won at other seats' auctions, and shipping earns cash. The island shaping term and the Shipper gate's primary metric (Island points) pay the bidder side; deliveries, island share and containers per delivery, the shipping side, only corroborate. *Measured* (gate, 300 games per seat; mean bids from `move_table.md`: Shippers $2.78-2.85, Peddler v2 $1.89, other nets $1.77-2.25):

- **The win-slack controller never engaged** (*measured*, `metrics.jsonl` of the runs, git-ignored). `dual_step` sets the floor to the mean of the first 6 iterations' training win minus `--win-slack` 0.05, raises the shaping coefficient while the training win is above it and lowers it while it is below, within [0, `--dual-max` 0.6]; `--shaping-weight 0.3` is only its starting value. That the high share contributes to the Shipper's win collapse is a *hypothesis*; no run used a lower ceiling. - **Games in which the harbor rule was waived (a one-off, not a property of the runs).** The engine waives all rules for a decision when every legal move is forbidden. `move_table.md` (harbor-gap line, *measured*) shows sails to a harbor exceeding harbor purchases for the uncapped Shippers, `shipper_s1` by 74 of 2314 sails (3.2%) and `shipper_s2` by 237 of 3307 (7.2%); for the capped Shippers by -19 of 2474 and 6 of 2614, and by zero for the other 11 nets bound by the rule. A one-off trace of `shipper_s2` (100 games per seat, not saved as a script) found 218 of its harbor sails opened no purchase, and in all 218 the legal moves were the three harbor sails, one Bank sail and one reprice, which is consistent with a waiver. *Ad hoc*, from an earlier unsaved analysis: 37 of `shipper_s2`'s 400 games and 17 of `shipper_s1`'s 400 had more harbor sails than purchases, and in those `shipper_s2` games final cash was about $0.76 (against $13.9 in its other games), the loan penalty 22 (against 5.3), passes 16.4 (against 1.5) and Island points 11 (against 66). No action-level replay was done, so the waiver is an inference. "The uncapped Shippers end games broke" therefore holds for this 4-9% of games and not for the runs in general; an earlier statement of the implementer's said otherwise and was too broad.

**Open questions for the independent review** (none is decided; 1-3 were proposed by the implementer and the user has not answered them).
1. Forced-build test: give each net a second factory and a second warehouse at the start, or force builds in evaluation, and see whether score, win or sales improve. If building pays and the nets still do not build, the cause is exploration or credit assignment; if it does not pay, the naive expectation is wrong for this ruleset.
2. Exploration for builds, only after 1. It must not pay the action itself (see Principle); it would have to pay the position that building creates.
3. Boat-fill test: how often a trip carries 3-5 containers, and whether a full ship pays more per sail than the same containers sailed one by one. A cargo-size histogram needs a new Rust counter and a change of the env's column count.
4. What the Shipper archetype means: the deliverer side (cash from deliveries, containers shipped) or the bidder side (Island points won). The current island term and gate reward the bidder side, and neither counts what the Island points cost.
5. A win guard measured at gate-like tables (Peddler v2, Consortium, Magnate), or a lower `--dual-max`, since the current guard never acted.
6. Gate against the matched control, and an effect-size floor on the Showcase flag; either changes the gate after seeing its results.
7. Keep the $5 floor of the bid cap or drop it, and whether to retrain the capped Shippers under the current rule (delete `runs/strategist/shipper_capped_s{1,2}` and run `bash reports/strategist/run_phase2.sh`, 30 minutes each, one at a time on this machine), given that the cap barely binds.
8. Fix `training/interplay.py` before Phase 5 (its rule binding, described there).
9. The stop rule: no archetype passes, so the plan says to rethink the rewards before spending the remaining 360 minutes. What to change first: the Producer terms (its sales share is still -0.35 / -0.16 SD against Peddler v2, though less negative than the controls' -0.88 / -0.54), the Shipper side, the win guard, or the Showcase flag.

Cash is one cause, and the minor one (about a quarter of the fall, see the lend run below): a bid is cash put on the table, a bidder who cannot afford its target bids all it has, and that share rises with the cargo: 10% of bidders for one container (mean target $8.1, cash $3.2), 35% for five (target $34.5, cash $14.4); those capped bidders bid about 0.4 of target, the others 1.0. The deliverer's cash is twice the highest bid, so the lost top bids cut it. The main cause is the assortment of the cargo (next bullet). Two earlier explanations were wrong or incomplete: falling marginal card values (wrong) and bidders' cash alone (incomplete).

With rivals able to pay, Peddler v2's delivery cash per game barely falls up to N=3 (188, 193, 191 at N=1-3; 159 at N=5); cash less purchases falls (121 to 76) with fewer trips, and win share still falls (0.86 to 0.63). Not tested: a one-colour cargo, or timing a big delivery for when rivals are flush (a human would).

## What we learned (summary of the probes, Step 1, Run A and the diagnosis)
Evidence tags: **measured** = evaluation data in `reports/strategist/`; **hypothesis** = not tested.

**Established (measured)**
- Nets can use extra factories; a factory pays for the control (win +0.126) but is neutral for Peddler v2. Warehouses are used but shift play from shipping to trading with no clear gain. No trained net ever holds more than its starting warehouse.
- 97-99% of trips carry one container. Cash per container falls with cargo size because of bidder cash (about a quarter of the fall) and the colour mix of a bigger cargo (the rest), not because of a rule. Waiting for a fuller ship does not pay against real bidders (delivery cash per game 102 -> 61 from 1 to 5 containers); with rich bidders it is neutral up to 3. One container per trip is not beaten, but not proven optimal either.
- A delivery pays the deliverer twice the top bid; Island points go to the winning bidder. The corrected Shipper reward (`--shaping-objective shipper`) pays the deliverer side. Run A (one seed) gave delivery net $20.2 against $10.4 for Peddler v2 and win 0.62, still with one container per trip. Part of that gain is bid inflation among learner copies: delivery cash and the seat's own bid money rose together (61 -> 100 and 64 -> 110).
- Reward accounting: it credits gross delivery cash and deducts only container purchases. Not in it: bids paid as a rival, Island points won, buy-outs, factory and warehouse costs, income from selling containers. `delivery_net` is therefore not profit (table in `diagnosis.md`).
- The win-slack controller never constrained anything: the shaping coefficient hit its 0.6 ceiling in every run, Run A included.
- At a table of complementary checkpoints (Producer, Showcase, control) the Shipper's delivery net does not improve beyond seed noise. Winning bids rise (3.5 -> 4.0-4.1) while rival cash falls, so demand is not just liquidity; supply worsens. Peddler v2 wins 0.07 there, with no seat artefact; it is out of distribution against its own descendants, and the control beats Producer and Showcase (0.46 against 0.27 and 0.21).
- "Competitive offers" is a stock level of cheap containers on a seat's lots, not a flow. It rose at the complementary table because cheap stock sits unsold, so it was a misleading sign of livelier trading.

**Hypotheses (not tested)**
- Colour 5 is scarce and drives Peddler v2's higher purchase price ($4.78 against $3.7-4.1). The counters have no price by colour or game stage.
- The one-container habit is inherited from Peddler v2; nothing in the reward makes waiting pay.
- A learner trained at a complementary table would behave differently from these fixed policies.
- Timing a big delivery for when rivals are flush, or shipping one-colour cargoes, might pay; the probes cannot value either.

**Unexplained**
- The focus Peddler v2 wins 0.18 from seat 1 and 0.53-0.66 from other seats at the current table.
- Producer and Showcase checkpoints are only modestly distinct from each other.

**Direction**
No further reward experiments for now. Next: heuristic-first, game-theory-informed architecture and cross-play experiments (EGTA Lite). Peddler v2, Magnate and Consortium stay external benchmarks, not regular members of the training ecosystem. All results and instrumentation are kept. The plan for this direction is "Phase H0" below.

## Phase H0 - heuristic-first opponents and EGTA Lite (no new rewards; the only training is the adaptive generalist below)

Peddler v2, Magnate and Consortium are external benchmarks only, not training opponents (Peddler v2's collapse against its own descendants is not evidence of strategic progress). No colour-by-stage instrumentation yet. No forced cargo size: the Shipper decides state by state whether to sail. The new bots are **not exposed** (`OPPONENTS` is unchanged, they are creatable by id only). Table size 5 is preferred but 3 and 4 are also played a lot, so every check covers 3, 4 and 5. Parameters are hand-set; tuning is deferred.

- Why the prices are low (traced in code and measured over 3,000 games per size, seats drawn at random from the three; the same at 3, 4 and 5 seats; no profile changed). The listing price of a heuristic comes only from `target_lot` in `archetype.rs`, which reads the profile's `factory_lot` and `harbor_lot` (a factory lot k costs $k+1, a harbor lot k costs $k+2): Producer 0 / 1 gives $1 at a factory and $3 at the harbor, Showcase 1 / 1 gives $2 and $3, Shipper 1 / 0 gives $2 and $2. It drops to the cheapest lot on the last turn, past 85% of the supply used, or after 4 idle decisions, and never raises a price. The valuation terms (`factory_unit`, `harbor_unit`, `variety`, `ship_taste`, `bid_share`) decide what to buy, build, ship and bid, never what to charge. So the answer to "shared valuation or profile weights" is neither: two hand-set lot constants plus the markdowns. Measured: the Producer sells 67-68% of its containers at $1 and 27-28% at $3 (mean $1.60-1.61); the Showcase about 60% at $3 and 26-30% at $2 (mean $2.51-2.59); the Shipper 41-49% at $2 from the harbor and 42-49% at $2 from a factory (mean $1.90-1.94). Markdowns put 7-23% of listings below the profile's lot (Shipper factory 15-23%, Producer harbor 13-19%, Showcase factory 9-15%, Showcase harbor 7-10%). A heuristic never meets a price above $3 in a heuristic-only table; the $4-6 prices come from the nets' relists, and only a Shipper pays them (by the code, not measured: it values the first container of an empty hold at about $8, the Producer values a restock at about $2).
